Mean |frame − previous| and "temporal noise" mean |frame − mean(neighbours)| (smooth motion cancels, aliasing
does not), 8-bit RGB units, divided by the volume's pixel fraction of the frame (the raw image means are diluted
by background). 240×135, froxels 96×96×16:

| Asset (dist) | dense (his mips) | `'none'` | `'coarse-pyramid'` | `'node-average'` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WDAS quarter (1.5) | — | 1.25 / 0.89 | 0.26 / 0.12 | **0.15 / 0.10** |
| smoke (2.5) | 0.10 / 0.08 | 0.31 / 0.19 | 0.09 / 0.08 | **0.06 / 0.06** |
| cloud04 (3) | 0.20 / 0.12 | 0.56 / 0.25 | 0.20 / 0.12 | **0.19 / 0.11** |

`'none'` aliases (3–7× the noise of the others on WDAS). The pyramid matches the dense mips' flicker; node averages
are a little calmer still, but they blur and brighten the volume: the leaf/lower averages spread density across
whole 8³/128³ cells (WDAS covers 4,924 px with node averages vs 2,213 with `'none'` and 2,441 with the pyramid;
35–37 dB vs dense at near range). That is the lower-node bias above plus the coarse lattice, not noise.

### HDDA on vs off

Images: identical in every mode (PSNR ∞ for pyramid and node, 100.5 dB / max diff 1/255 for `'none'`, from the
computed vs accumulated sample position; 0 px over 8/255). Probe: 512 `(ts, te)` pairs (plain and dilated)
match the CPU twin exactly (0 mismatches).

Froxel (inject) pass, median of 15 after 3 warm-up, ms, **provisional (D25)**, paired in one page:

| Scene | `'none'` hdda / no | `'coarse-pyramid'` hdda / no | dense |
|---|---|---|---|
| WDAS, 96²×16, 16 steps | 12.1 / 11.7 | 26.5 / 22.3 | — |
| smoke, 96²×16, 16 steps | 5.1 / 4.4 | 11.7 / 9.9 | 1.4 |
| bunny 128²×24, 32 steps (no hdda variant) | 85.4 | 195.3 | 17.8 (`'node-average'` 431.6) |

On SwiftShader the skip never paid: its loop structure (a `while` with computed positions) costs more than the
zero samples it removes, even after making it lazy (ask only after a zero sample) and turning it off for steps
> 8 voxels. Hence **`hdda` defaults to false**; it stays built and gated so the hardware rerun (K3.2c) can flip it.
R1's expectation (largest gain in the 32-step shadow march of sparse assets) needs a real GPU, where divergent
8-tap loads, not ALU, dominate. Also from these numbers: sparse level 0 is ~5× the dense hardware-trilinear cost
here, and node averages ~5× `'none'` (8 tree walks + a 16-word popcount per tap).

## Recommended default: `lod: 'coarse-pyramid'` (now the default of `vdbSparseSource`)

Pros: closest to his LOD (50–68 dB vs his mips on identical voxels), flicker equal to the dense path (4–7× less
than `'none'` on WDAS), the cheapest coarse taps (one hardware-filtered r16f fetch; ~2.2× `'none'` in the froxel
pass vs ~5× for node averages), small memory (0.6 MiB for WDAS, ~2% of the grid). Cons: needs the density image on
the CPU and a CPU resample at creation (K3.2a moves it to the GPU); the pyramid is static (a `pool.replace` needs a
rebuild); levels 1…L₀−1 are a blend toward L₀, not true box levels. `'node-average'` stays for memory-tight or
image-less cases (no CPU copy, no extra memory) with its bias documented; `'none'` for parity and debugging.
Settled by: the hardware rerun of (d) and the per-pass ms (K3.2c); if node averages turn out cheap on hardware,
fixing their lower-node bias (an active count per lower node at build time) would make them the zero-memory pick.

   (level 0 only), `'coarse-pyramid'` (default: dense r16f levels from L₀ = max(1, ⌈log2(maxTexels/pyramidMax)⌉),
   base = box resample of the grid, levels above = his atlas in float; levels 1…L₀−1 blend level 0 toward L₀),
   `'node-average'` (leaf and lower-node averages at levels 3 and 7; the lower level is the mean of active voxels).
   Reason: NanoVDB has no mips (R1 §4.3). Revisit: K3.2c hardware A/B; K3.2a GPU pyramid build.
3. mjurczyk `FrameInjectMaterial` shadow march (fixed steps, accumulated probe) → with a sparse source and

   bound) and positions computed per step (float noise vs his accumulation; images identical to ≤ 1/255). Off by
   default (it did not pay on SwiftShader). Reason: R1 §4.3 HDDA gains. Revisit: K3.2c.
4. mjurczyk `FrameVolume` mean density from 8-bit texels (or baked header stats) → the sparse source's mean of the
   grid's non-zero voxel values (f32), or caller `stats`. Reason: no texels. Revisit: never.
5. mjurczyk aux grids (mask/albedo/emission at the density's size and mips) → sparse aux grids are separate pool
   grids with their own index boxes, read at level 0 only; albedo is one grey grid or three scalar grids (the pool
