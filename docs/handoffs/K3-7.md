## The tracer

Index space, one accessor, per ray:

1. Clip `[t0, t1]` to the trilinear support box `[min - 1, max + 1]`.
2. HDDA over aligned cells of at least 8 voxels; the cell size follows `vdbGetDim` at the cell corner (8 in leaves
   and lower tiles, 128/4096 in upper/root tiles and the background).
3. **Skip test** per cell: the 2x2x2 block of same-size cells from its corner (every tap a trilinear sample in
   the cell can read) is all uniform (`vdbGetDim >= dim`) with values `> 0`. A level set's tiles and background
   are `±background` (K4.8/K4.9), so outside space is skipped and inside tiles never are. A large cell that fails
   is walked again at 8 (`fineUntil`).
4. Other cells: sphere trace, step `max(0.95 · v · sdfToIndex, 0.25)` voxels (his min-step rule; the SDF is in
   the grid's world units, `sdfToIndex = 1 / voxelSize`, which the material reads from `pool.info(id)`).
5. The first sample `< 0` brackets the crossing with the last sample `>= 0`: 8 bisections, then a secant step (his
   secant step, plus the bisection). A ray whose first sample is inside hits there (his behaviour).
6. Normal: central differences of `vdbSample` at `±normalStep` (default 1) voxels, world normal `normalize(W^T g)`.

The material traces from the pixel, not from the box: the view ray comes from `viewportUV` through
`cameraProjectionMatrixInverse` (perspective from `cameraPosition`, orthographic along the view axis for
directional-light shadow maps). His `positionViewDirection` + back-face `positionLocal` ray moves with the
varying precision of the large box faces: ~0.02 px, which put GPU hits up to 4 voxels off the CPU twin's at
192x108 (measured before the change: IoU 0.991, hit max 4.17 voxels; after: IoU 1, 0.0016 voxels).

Marching-cubes mesh (`LevelSetMesh`): **not ported**. R1 §7 lists his `marchingCubesTables.js` (Paul Bourke's
tables, no stated licence) as not ported in K3, and the trace replaces it for display. Suggestion for a follow-up
if a mesh is wanted (collision, export): a table-free surface-nets mesh over the leaves in `tools` (one vertex per
crossing cell, no 256-case table), tested against the trace's hits (vertex distance to the zero set < 0.5 voxel)
and the sphere's analytic radius. Pro: no table provenance question, works on the sparse tree at full resolution.
Con: quads, not his triangles; the mesh A/B against his then compares two different meshers.

## DIVERGES text (added as V56–V59)

1. **mjurczyk `levelset/webgpu/LevelSetSurfaceMaterial.js` (sphere trace of a dense 8-bit SDF texture, resolution
   200 on the longest side; 4x4x4 box filter at crossing texels)** → `VDBLevelSetMaterial` traces the sparse
   NanoVDB level set at full resolution: core helper `vdbLevelSetHit` (HDDA skip of uniform tile/background cells
   above zero, sphere trace with his minimum step, 8 bisections + his secant step). Why: the sparse core (D18);
   no 8-bit banding; no CPU resample. Cost: the full-resolution grid in the pool (teapot 81 MB Float).
2. **His ray (`positionViewDirection` + back-face `positionLocal`)** → the pixel's ray through the inverse
   projection (perspective from the camera; orthographic along the view axis). Why: the interpolated box
   positions moved the ray ~0.02 px (4 voxels on grazing hits vs the CPU twin).
3. **His normal (central difference at one dense texel of the 8-bit texture)** → central differences of the
   full-resolution trilinear SDF at `normalStep` index voxels (default 1), through `W^T` of the grid's affine.
   Sharper than his (his texel is ~5 teapot voxels); normal-angle differences vs his are expected at his
   texel scale.
4. **His placement (an Object3D transform on a box in the grid's world space)** → the grid placed with
   `pool.setTransform`, the proxy `vdbProxyGeometry(info, { pad: 1 })` with an identity mesh. Same shading
   (`MeshStandardNodeMaterial`, mask, depth, received-shadow position, `positionView` moved to the hit).
5. **His `LevelSetMesh` (CPU marching cubes, Paul Bourke tables)** → not ported (table provenance, R1 §7; the
   trace replaces it for display). Revisit: a table-free surface-nets mesh in `tools` if a mesh is wanted.
6. **His `VDBPoints` / `createPointData`** → `VDBPoints` takes `vdbPointRenderData` (tools, from our K4.2 point
   decode, one pass, no time slicing); spacing uses the grid's active voxel count (OpenVDB point grids: the
   voxels with points) where he counts voxels with `end > start`; the pixel scale reads the render target's
   height when one is set (his: `renderer.getSize`); no `.wvdb` points LOD streaming (`update()` with growing
   arrays) yet.
7. **Harness only**: his normals captures are taken with his public three API (emissive normal, lights hidden),
   not a code change; our harness renders the same encoding.
